engine engine/src/ — the pure game, zero dependencies, zero I/O

wall.ts36

The 144-tile wall, shuffled by a seeded PRNG (mulberry32). Same seed → same wall, which is what makes every hand reproducible, every replay exact, and a fairness proof possible (publish a hash of the seed before the hand, reveal it after).

ready.ts verified 800/800133

Distance-to-ready (上聽): how many tiles away a hand is from a winning shape, and which tiles get it there. Two real bugs were found and fixed here by an independent exhaustive checker — a 14-tile-shape miscount and a subtle proxy-metric bug in the decomposition. Now agrees with brute force on every sampled hand. ~7µs per call, which is what makes the ML loop affordable.

decompose.ts246

Breaks a winning 14 tiles into 4 sets + pair — returning every valid reading, not just one, because a hand can be read multiple ways and the scorer must pick the highest. This is the piece the old Python engine lacked entirely (it assumed a concealed hand; a kong crashed it).

scoring.ts 4-way verified345

The faan engine. Scores every decomposition against the pattern catalogue, applies subsumption (so 大三元 never also pays 小三元 — including your 2026-08-26 rulings), breaks ties deterministically, adds situational faan (自摸, 搶槓, 槓上開花, heavenly/earthly, flowers), caps at 13, and refuses wins under the 3-faan floor. Verified by: the golden suite, an independent second implementation (40k hands agree), the Python port-diff, and the audit.

reducer.ts the heart1,382

The match state machine — DESIGN.md §5.2 implemented exactly. Deal → recursive flower replacement (strictly ordered) → turns → claim windows with win > kong/pung > chow priority → kongs and replacements → scoring → settlement → dealer repeat/rotation → wind rounds. Pure function: applyAction(state, action) → {state, events}. The Durable Object, replay viewer, bots and sim all call this same function — nothing else contains game logic.

startMatch · applyAction · legalActions · startNextHand · replayMatch (re-execution — the property test folds 200 hands' logs back through and demands identical state)
bots.ts the product at P01,048

The opponents. Picks a faan route at deal (nine templates: half flush, all pungs, honours…), steers discards and claims toward it, never melds into a hand that can't reach 3 faan, uses count-based safety. All behaviour flows through BotProfile — 11 numeric weights, which is exactly what the evolution loop tunes. Sees only SeatView (its own redacted view) — it cannot cheat by construction. Known gaps, from your interview: no information warfare, no left-feed awareness, no 13-orphans route.

Every game event, versioned, with engineVersion pinned so a future scoring fix can never silently rewrite history. The 11 bot weights engine/src/bots.ts BotProfile — what the ML loop actually tunes

The bot makes three kinds of decision — which route to build, which tile to cut, whether to claim — each scored by a small formula. Every coefficient in those formulas is a weight. Evolution never invents behaviour; it only re-balances these dials.
Route choicescore = faan·w − distance·w − surplus·w − floorPenalty

faanWeight (default 0.6) — how much a route's payout matters vs how fast it completes. Low = race for any legal 3; high = build 半flush/dragons value. This is your "big hands always lose, small hands always lose" dial — the mix lives here.

routeDistanceWeight (1.0) — the speed term: penalty per tile the route still needs. High = sprint to ready; low = patient.

offRouteWeight (1.2) — penalty per concealed tile the route would throw away. High = prefer routes that use what you were dealt; low = happy to demolish and rebuild (your "dump a suit" rescue is cheap when this is low).

belowMinimumPenalty (5) — flat charge on any route that cannot legally reach 3 faan. The anti-sin dial — directly controls the refused-win pathology. Both evolution runs pushed it up (5 → 6.3 → 8.7): chip pressure agrees with the rule.

Discard choicescore = −distance·w − routeDist·w ± routeFit·w − danger·w + outs·w

discardDistanceWeight (3.0) — keep the hand close to ready: penalty for a cut that moves you further away. The dominant term, and rightly so.

discardRouteWeight (1.15) — prefer cuts that serve the chosen route (shed off-route tiles, keep route tiles). This is "commitment": high = tunnel vision, low = stay flexible.

discardSafetyWeight (0.45) — count-based danger: prefer tiles with more copies already visible. Your interview says defence should scale with the opponent threat and your own hand size — today it's a flat dial, which is one of the named gaps.

discardOutsWeight (0.055) — tiny tiebreak by live-tile count between otherwise equal cuts. Deliberately small: §6 says outs are a tiebreak, not a driver.

Claim choicerefuse if routeLoss > tolerance · claim if gain ≥ bar·(2−aggression)

claimRouteTolerance (1.6) — how much route value a pung/chow may destroy before it's refused. Claiming always costs concealment; this decides when that price is worth paying. Run 1 cut it 40% — the optimizer discovering your "beginners claim too much."

claimSpeedGain (1.0) — the minimum distance improvement a chow/pung must buy. Higher = only claim when it genuinely accelerates the hand.

aggression (1.0) — a global multiplier on claim willingness, scaling the bar and the gain together. The one personality-flavoured dial; also the noisiest in both runs (it wandered ±80%), suggesting the game is less sensitive to it than the others.

How this variable set was chosen — and why not more or fewer. The honest answer: the architecture came first, from DESIGN.md §6's priority list (claim logic → route steering → count safety → outs tiebreak), which itself came from the engine audit's diagnosis of why naive bots are unbearable. The builder wrote one additive scoring formula per decision, and then every coefficient in those formulas became a weight — no more, no less. So the set isn't derived from data; it's the full dial-set of a hand-designed heuristic.

Why not fewer: collapsing dials (say, one "speed vs value" knob) would couple decisions that your own play keeps separate — you can be patient in route choice yet ruthless in claims. Why not more: two reasons. Search cost — at 48 matches per candidate, 11 dimensions is already near what the noise budget supports; every added dial slows convergence for all of them. And the deeper one: weights can only re-balance what the formulas can see. The bot's inputs today are its own hand, the discards, and visible counts. No weight setting can make it bluff, watch the left player's feed, or model opponents — those need new features (new inputs and terms), which is exactly what STRATEGY.md's gap table lists. When those land, each brings its own weight, and the search space grows because the behaviour space grew — the right order, not the reverse.

Run-3 evidence so far supports the design: the dials that matter (floor penalty, claim discipline, faan value) move consistently across runs; the ones that wander (aggression, safety) are either insensitive or waiting on missing features.
