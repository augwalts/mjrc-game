Game stats — real tester data

Every number here comes from the live database: five matches from four testers, 22 hands and 447 graded moves. The per-hand figures are not stored anywhere — they are recovered by replaying each match's actions_gz through the reducer, which is what makes charts 2 and 3 possible without a migration.

Drawn the way the site draws: hand-rolled inline SVG, no library, never two y-scales on one chart, faint line per match with the aggregate bold on top.

1 · Score progression

Running chip totals after every hand, one line per seat. You are the gold line.
From the replayed standings array. This is the chart chart.ts::progressionSvg already draws on the site — worth porting rather than rewriting.

2 · Hand distribution

How big the winning hands were, in faan.
Split by ruleset, deliberately. MJRC standard has a 3-faan floor and TVB-2026 has a 1-faan floor, so their hands live in different ranges — 3 to 9 against 1 to 2. Pooling them would draw a bimodal hump that describes the rule sets, not the players. This is the same reason the site prices everything per game's average winning hand.

3 · Agreement by decision difficulty

How often you played the engine's own top choice — split by how much better that choice was than its second.
The one metric the website can never compute — and the measured shape is a U, which is not what I expected. Testers play the obvious moves right (94%) and diverge on the close ones (29%), and the near-tie band is half of all the moves ever graded. So the single headline number is 49% while agreement on decisions that actually cost something is 69%.

I had assumed a raw match rate would flatter players by counting forced moves as wins. On this data it does the opposite: it understates them, because half of every game is near-ties where diverging from the engine is worth almost nothing by definition. Whether that holds up past four testers is exactly the kind of thing more data settles.

4 · Where the errors are

Cost of a choice against the engine's pick, in its own units.
Zero means you matched it. The story is the tail, not the peak.

Biggest gaps across all five matches — what was played, and what the engine would have played instead.
Almost certainly the most useful thing here for an actual player, and it needs no replay and no migration — game_move is already queryable.
