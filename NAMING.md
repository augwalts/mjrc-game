# Naming — the game

**Status:** 2026-08-31 — English name **shortlisted: Mahjong Movement**. The Chinese
mark is still open: 雀派 reads as "sparrow school" in Mandarin and is superseded if
Mandarin reach matters (see §8). No trademark or domain check has been run.

- **English (shortlisted):** Mahjong Movement
- **Chinese mark (open):** 新派 leading · 同台 strongest alternative · 雀派 if HK-only

Reads against `POSITIONING-AMENDMENT.md` (2026-08-26) throughout — several
candidates were killed by that document rather than by taste.

---

## 1. The brief

From the owner, verbally, plus what the positioning doc already settled:

- **Strong AI, genuinely competitive** — bots are the P0 product
- **Community** — the LA scene: parking lot, beach, fancy table, family table
- **Art and craft** — carvers, art movements as tile registers, artist-credited sets
- **Warm, but not saccharine** — "Mahjong Family" was rejected as too warm
- **Not nostalgic** — heritage is one register among several, never the house style
- **Multicultural, fusion-y, fun**
- **HK is pragmatism. Mahjong is strategy, personalities, art and science — a mixture.**
- **Must contain MJ or Mahjong**
- **Must work in both Chinese and English**

Hard constraint from the amendment's §7: this is **fun-first, not research-first.**
Priority order is fun, retention, everything else. Any name that fronts study or
optimisation is wrong regardless of how good it sounds.

## 2. The recommendation

### 雀派 / Mahjong Movement

Full Chinese form **麻雀派**; **雀派** is the two-character contraction and the
better wordmark.

> **Superseded for tri-lingual use.** The analysis below holds and 雀派 remains the
> best HK-only mark, but 雀 means mahjong in Cantonese and Japanese only — Mandarin
> reads it as *sparrow*. See §8.

### Character by character

| Character | Jyutping | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 雀 | zoek3 | sparrow — and the standard HK shorthand for mahjong |
| 派 | paai3 | school, faction, movement |

**Literal, character for character:** "sparrow school" / "sparrow faction."

**Idiomatic:** "the mahjong school" / "the mahjong movement."

### Why 雀 means mahjong

麻雀 (maa4 zoek3) is the Cantonese word for the game and literally means *sparrow*.
雀 alone is established HK shorthand — 打雀 (play mahjong), 雀館 (parlour), 雀友
(mahjong buddies), 雀局 (a session). In a game context nobody reads it as a bird.

Why sparrow: usually attributed either to the clatter of tiles sounding like
sparrows, or to the bird on the 一索 tile. See
`reference/mahjong-treasures/text/2014-05-16-a-theory-about-the-evolution-of-the-one-bam-tile.txt`.

### Why 派 is the right character

Four live senses, all of them useful:

1. **School or movement, art-historical.** 畫派 school of painting · 流派 school,
   current · 海上畫派 the Shanghai School. This is the exact word for what the tile
   sets are.
2. **To deal.** 派牌 = to deal tiles or cards. Already inside mahjong's vocabulary.
3. **To hand out, distribute.** 派錢. Reads generous, communal.
4. **Style, panache.** 派頭 in HK Cantonese = swagger, having style.

雀派 is a coinage, but a HK reader parses it instantly on the model of 畫派 and
學派. It lands as "the mahjong school" without effort.

### The pun

派 is **paai3**. 牌 is **paai4** — *tile*.

麻雀牌 / 雀牌 means *mahjong tiles*, an everyday word. So 雀派 sits one tone step
off 雀牌. Spoken, a listener hears "mahjong tiles" and then registers "school."

Cantonese distinguishes tones cleanly, so this reads as deliberate wordplay rather
than ambiguity — which is the desired outcome. **The name sounds like the object
the game is played with.**

### The English side

"Movement" carries four senses, and the product uses all four:

1. **Art movement** — Bauhaus, De Stijl, the Shanghai School. The tile sets exactly.
2. **Social movement** — a body of people with momentum. Community, joining.
3. **Physical motion** — tiles in transit, toss styles, 洗牌 and 開牌, the 3D layer.
4. **Musical movement** — a section of a larger work. Craft and structure.

It is also forward-facing by definition, which is the direct answer to "not
nostalgic." A movement is something happening now; heritage names all point back.

### How the two halves relate

**Parallel names, not translations.** They meet on the art sense — the load-bearing
one — and each then carries something native to its own language:

- 派 adds *dealing tiles* and *style*, which English cannot hold
- Movement adds *momentum* and *motion*, which 派 cannot hold

A literal translation would give 麻雀運動, which reads as "mahjong athletics."
Dead on arrival. **Do not translate the name.**

### Saying it

**雀派** — *jeuk paai*, Jyutping `zoek3 paai3`. Two syllables, both easy for an
English speaker.

## 3. Is there a single word for "movement"?

Yes — **流派** (lau4 paai3), the precise term for a school or movement in arts and
letters.

| Word | Jyutping | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| **流派** | lau4 paai3 | school, movement, current — the exact art-historical term |
| 派 | paai3 | school or faction; needs context alone |
| 潮流 | ciu4 lau4 | trend, tide — fashion-facing |
| 風潮 | fung1 ciu4 | a wave or groundswell; often agitation |
| 運動 | wan6 dung6 | social/sports movement — reads *athletics*. Avoid. |

流派 is the correct concept word but not a mark — alone it says "school" and
nothing about mahjong.

## 4. Four-character forms

Chinese brand names sit naturally at two or four characters; four gives a 2+2
rhythm with classical weight.

**麻雀流派** — maa4 zoek3 lau4 paai3
Literally "Mahjong Movement." Most faithful, keeps the 派/牌 pun. Slightly formal —
reads like an academic category.

**新派麻雀** — san1 paai3 maa4 zoek3 — *"new-school mahjong"* — **preferred**
新派 is live vocabulary for a modern reinterpretation of a tradition: 新派粵菜
(new-style Cantonese cuisine), 新派武俠 (new-school wuxia). Every HK reader parses
it instantly.

Critically, **it states the anti-nostalgia position inside the name.** 新派 exists
only in opposition to 舊派 (gau6 paai3, old school), so the break with heritage is
built into the grammar rather than asserted.

The precedent that does the work is **新派武俠** — New School Wuxia, the 1950s-70s
movement of 金庸, 梁羽生 and 古龍. It took a calcified traditional genre, modernised
the prose and psychology, absorbed Western and Japanese technique, was commercially
popular rather than academic, revered the form without becoming a museum, and came
out of Hong Kong (Jin Yong serialised in 明報, which he founded). An educated Chinese
speaker hearing 新派麻雀 thinks 新派武俠 and gets the whole positioning unprompted.

**The pun does NOT survive here.** 雀派 works because 雀 is directly followed by
*paai*, mirroring 雀牌. Neither 新派麻雀 (新-派-麻-雀) nor 麻雀新派 (麻-雀-新-派) puts
雀 next to 派. The pun exists only in 雀派 and 麻雀派.

Its weakness is that it is descriptive — a category, not a name — and it pairs more
loosely with "Movement" than 雀派 does.

**麻雀新派** — same characters reversed. Reads as "the new school *of* mahjong" —
more brand-like, less idiomatic.

**These coexist.** 雀派 is the logo; 新派麻雀 is the descriptor line.

## 5. Lockups

Standard HK practice is Chinese primary, English secondary.

Stacked — Chinese as mark, English as descriptor:

```
    雀 派
MAHJONG MOVEMENT
```

Rule-separated horizontal:

```
雀派 │ MAHJONG MOVEMENT
```

Two-line, English-led, for Western-facing surfaces:

```
MAHJONG
MOVEMENT   雀派
```

The rule-separated form is more than a layout convention. The thesis is
*incompatible registers sharing one stage*, so a rule between a Chinese mark and a
Latin one **is** the argument. Set 雀派 in a brush or woodblock register and
MAHJONG MOVEMENT in Bauhaus grotesque and the lockup demonstrates the product.

### The test that matters

**The wordmark must be re-settable in every register shipped as a tile set** —
青花 porcelain, HK neon, 月份牌, De Stijl, woodblock, Cantonese opera. If it holds
in all of them, the logo demonstrates the cosmetic system rather than sitting
beside it. No other candidate offered this.

## 8. Working across English, Cantonese and Mandarin

### The problem is vocabulary, not characters

Most characters carry the same meaning in both Chinese languages — 派 is pài /
paai3 = school in both, 新派 is xīn pài / san1 paai3 in both. The break is one word:

| | Cantonese | Mandarin |
|---|---|---|
| 麻雀 | **mahjong** (maa4 zoek3) | **sparrow only** (máquè) |
| 麻將 | understood, not the local word | **mahjong** (májiàng) |

雀 as shorthand for mahjong is Cantonese and Japanese. A Mandarin reader sees 雀派
and gets "sparrow school" — not wrong given the etymology, but not the name.

### The rule

**Avoid the mahjong word in the mark entirely.** Build on characters whose meaning
is identical in both languages — 牌 (tile), 派 (school), and the direction and wind
characters. Let the English half carry the category.

### The 新派 solution

Keep the mark stable; localize the category word in the descriptor.

| | Mark | Descriptor | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| HK / Cantonese | **新派** | 新派麻雀 | Mahjong Movement |
| Mandarin | **新派** | 新派麻將 | Mahjong Movement |

新派 reads identically in both, and the 新派武俠 lineage is *larger* in Mandarin
markets than in HK — Jin Yong is foundational across the mainland and Taiwan. The
strongest thing about the name gets stronger, not weaker. Cost: 新派 alone is
generic and needs the English or descriptor beside it.

### Cross-language alternatives

All read identically in Mandarin and Cantonese.

**Sharing one surface**

| 中文 | Mandarin | Cantonese | Literal | English pairing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **同台** | tóng tái | tung4 toi4 | same stage / same table | One Table · One Stage |
| **東西** | dōng xī | dung1 sai1 | east west | East West |
| **四方** | sì fāng | sei3 fong1 | four directions | Four Winds |
| **四海** | sì hǎi | sei3 hoi2 | four seas | Four Seas |
| **八方** | bā fāng | baat3 fong1 | eight directions | All Quarters |

**同台 is the standout.** Mandarin: *to appear on the same stage* (同台演出).
Cantonese: 同枱, *the same table* (同枱食飯). The amendment's central sentence is
"incompatible visual registers sharing one stage" — the Mandarin reading is that
verbatim, the Cantonese reading is the mahjong table. Two different correct answers
in one mark.

**東西** is East + West (the fusion thesis), 東 and 西 are two wind tiles, and 東西
colloquially means "thing." Note 不是東西 is an insult.

**Built on 牌 — universal for *tile***

| 中文 | Mandarin | Cantonese | Literal | English pairing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **牌友** | pái yǒu | paai4 jau5 | tile friends | Mahjong Company |
| **牌局** | pái jú | paai4 guk6 | the tile session | The Session |
| **牌派** | pái pài | paai4 paai3 | tile school | Mahjong Movement |
| **牌藝** | pái yì | paai4 ngai6 | tile art, tile skill | Mahjong Craft |
| **牌風** | pái fēng | paai4 fung1 | tile luck, tile style | Mahjong Style |
| **牌會** | pái huì | paai4 wui2 | tile society | Mahjong Guild |
| **牌聚** | pái jù | paai4 zeoi6 | the tile gathering | Mahjong Meet |
| **潮牌** | cháo pái | ciu4 paai4 | trend tiles | Mahjong Wave |

牌友 and 牌局 are real words in *both* languages — unlike 雀友, which is
Cantonese-only. 牌藝 carries craft and excellence at once (藝 = art *and* skill).
潮牌 is Mandarin slang for a streetwear label, so it reads "trendy brand" and
"trendy tiles" together.

**Built on 派 — universal for *school, movement***

| 中文 | Mandarin | Cantonese | Literal | English pairing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **新派** | xīn pài | san1 paai3 | new school | Mahjong Movement |
| **流派** | liú pài | lau4 paai3 | movement, current | Mahjong Movement |
| **匠派** | jiàng pài | zoeng6 paai3 | craftsmen's school | Mahjong Guild |
| **藝派** | yì pài | ngai6 paai3 | art school | Mahjong Movement |
| **門派** | mén pài | mun4 paai3 | sect, lineage | Mahjong Guild |

匠 is the character for an artisan, so 匠派 literally names the carvers — the
artist-commissioned model in two characters. 門派 is the wuxia term for a
martial-arts school and inherits the 新派武俠 lineage directly.

**Craft and gathering**

| 中文 | Mandarin | Cantonese | Literal | English pairing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **工坊** | gōng fāng | gung1 fong1 | workshop | Mahjong Works |
| **百花** | bǎi huā | baak3 faa1 | hundred flowers | Hundred Flowers |

坊 means both *workshop* and *neighbourhood block* (作坊, 街坊) — craft and community
in one character.

**百花 carries a political risk.** 百花齊放 is the idiom for a flourishing of diverse
art and 花 is the flower tiles, but the Hundred Flowers Campaign of 1956-57 ended in
purges and that association is live in mainland markets. Only viable if mainland
reach is permanently off the table.

### Standouts

**同台** (states the thesis in both languages, differently and correctly in each) ·
**牌友** (a real warm word, not a coinage) · **匠派** (craft leading).
