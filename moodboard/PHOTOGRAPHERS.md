# Colour photographers — hunting list

**Date:** 2026-09-01 · You asked: *"it might be worth trying to find famous photographers
who've photographed very colourful images of these places."* You're right, and there's a
structural reason it matters.

## Why the board needs this

Everything collected so far came from **Wikimedia Commons**, because it's the only large
image source that is openly licensed, programmatically queryable, and carries attribution.
But Commons skews **documentary and utilitarian** — people photograph things there to
*record* them, not to make them beautiful. That is precisely why raw palette extraction
returned grey mud, and why the neon in the collection is mostly snapshots rather than
compositions.

**None of the photographers below are on Commons.** Their work is copyrighted and I can't
collect it. This is a manual hunting list — books to buy, galleries to visit, exhibitions to
see. For a private moodboard, saving reference images you've found is normal practice; just
keep them out of anything you publish.

---

## Hong Kong

**Greg Girard** — *the* name for what you're describing. Canadian, arrived in HK 1974, based
there from 1982, spent thirty years shooting Asia at night. Renowned specifically for his eye
for colour — neon-saturated night streets, lit interiors, the city before demolition. Also
co-author of *City of Darkness* (Kowloon Walled City). **Start here.** Books: *HK:PM*,
*City of Darkness*, *Under Vancouver*.

**Michael Wolf** (1954–2019) — German, worked two registers: *Architecture of Density*
(building facades as flat abstract colour fields — genuinely close to a Mondrian grid made of
real windows) and *Hong Kong Informal Solutions* / back-alley work (vernacular improvisation,
saturated close-up detail). M+ holds his work.

**Romain Jacquet-Lagrèze** — French, HK-based, born 1987. *Vertical Horizon* (looking up
between towers), *The Blue Moment* (dusk colour), *Wild Concrete* (trees growing out of
buildings — literally your density-vs-jungle contrast). Contemporary, very colour-forward.

**Christophe Jacrot** — HK in rain. Wet streets, reflected neon, colour through water. Useful
for the specific condition where HK looks best.

**Keith Macgregor** — vintage HK colour, 1970s–80s. Junks, streets, signage before the
clean-up.

**Fan Ho (何藩)** — already in `inspiration.md`, but note: **he is almost entirely
black-and-white.** Canonical for light, geometry and compression — not a colour reference.
Worth being clear about that so you don't hunt him for the wrong thing.

**Blue Lotus Gallery (HK)** — the practical hub. Represents Fan Ho, Girard and
Jacquet-Lagrèze, runs street-photography exhibitions, sells the books. If you're in HK this is
one stop for most of this list.

## Singapore

**Nguan** — the strongest match for your Singapore read. Born and raised there; famous for a
distinct **pastel palette with a signature "Singapore Pink"** tint, shot in the last two hours
of daylight, deliberately simulating the palette of coloured pencils. He avoids the downtown
and shoots the quieter HDB city. His stated aim is to make every setting look like a painted
theatrical set — which is *exactly* the register a stylised game wants. Work is in the
Singapore Art Museum's permanent collection. Book: *How Loneliness Goes*.

**Aik Beng Chia** — Singapore street, grittier and more immediate counterweight to Nguan.

## Tokyo

**Liam Wong** — double relevance: he was a **games art director at Ubisoft** before publishing
*TO:KY:OO*, saturated neon Tokyo at night graded like a game. The single most directly useful
name on this list for you, because he's already translating between the two disciplines.

**Masashi Wakui** — Tokyo backstreets and neon, heavily graded, cinematic colour.

## Colour masters worth studying for method, not subject

- **Alex Webb** (Magnum) — layered, complex compositions in intense colour and hard light. The
  benchmark for making saturated colour *readable* rather than chaotic — which is your tile
  problem stated as photography.
- **Harry Gruyaert** (Magnum) — colour as the primary subject; Morocco, Egypt, India.
- **Ernst Haas** — the colour pioneer; motion and abstraction.
- **Hiroji Kubota** (Magnum) — decades of colour work across China and Asia.
- **Bruno Barbey** (Magnum) — colour China, 1970s–80s.
- **Saul Leiter** — NY, not Asia, but the muted-colour-through-glass-and-rain approach maps
  directly onto wet HK streets.

## Where to actually get the images

1. **Books** — the reliable route. Girard's *HK:PM*, Wolf's *Architecture of Density*,
   Nguan's *How Loneliness Goes*, Wong's *TO:KY:OO*.
2. **Blue Lotus Gallery** (HK) — exhibitions and prints for the HK names.
3. **M+ Museum** — Wolf and HK design/photography holdings; the neon-sign archive is already
   in `inspiration.md`.
4. **Magnum Photos** — Webb, Gruyaert, Kubota, Barbey; browsable online.
5. **Instagram** — most contemporary names (Jacquet-Lagrèze, Wakui, Wong, Aik Beng Chia) post
   regularly, and it's the fastest way to decide who's worth a book.

## Suggested next step

Rather than collecting broadly from these, pick **two or three** and study them properly —
I'd suggest **Girard** (HK night colour), **Nguan** (Singapore pastel), and **Liam Wong**
(games-adjacent neon). Drop frames into `photographers/<name>/` and they'll appear in the
viewer automatically, with palettes extracted, so you can compare their palettes directly
against the measured city palettes in `PALETTE.md`. That comparison — *how a great colourist
grades this city vs. how the city actually measures* — is probably the most useful art-direction
exercise available to you right now.
