# Era 2 — Review (2026-08-28)

Status first: era 2 is **concluded** — 18 cycles (~11.5h), king frozen as
`tools/sim/baseline-v2.json`. Era 3 launched 2026-08-28 12:14 PM
(`--era 3 --enemy baseline-v2 --seed-profile era3-start --fitness chips`) and is
on cycle 1 now. `STATUS.md` still shows era-2 text only because era 3 hasn't
finished a cycle yet.

## 1. What era 2 actually was (correcting the framing)

"We gave it a harder opponent that can defend" is half right. The important
nuance:

- **A hand-built defender was tried first and lost.** The "Defender-baseline
  tournament" (2026-08-27) took DEFAULT + threat dials (`cand-defender.json`,
  sens 1.0 / push 0.8, plus a cautious variant) and benched it: **−21.1 vs
  baseline-v0**, and −51.4 when one defender sat with three champions. Bolt-on
  defense on an otherwise-default profile is a net loser.
- **So the era-2 enemy is not "a defender" — it's the era-1 champion**, frozen
  as `baseline-v1`. It *does* defend (threatSensitivity 0.96, push 0.76,
  urgency 0.42, suitContest 0.89 — all live), but its character per the era-1
  post-mortem is aggressive: faanWeight +70%, aggression +65%, safety −33% vs
  default — "wins by winning bigger, not folding." Era 2 is harder mainly
  because the opponent is *good*, of which defense is one modest part.
- **King-of-the-hill design:** enemy and seed are the same profile
  (baseline-v1), so 0 chips/match = par and any positive hall-of-fame score is
  genuine progress over the previous champion.

Two other things changed at the era boundary and are confounded with "harder
opponent" in any era-1 → era-2 comparison:

1. **Ruleset:** mjrc-standard (3–10 faan cap) replaced hkos (3–13).
2. **Bench fix:** the seat-luck bias (fixed seed↔seat pairing baking wall luck
   into benchmarks) was found and fixed at era-2 start — every wall is now
   played from all four chairs. This retroactively deflated era 1: the +70.2
   peak was partly block luck; bias-free re-measure put the champion at
   +33.5/+40.9 vs v0.

## 2. Harness mechanics (per `overnight.mjs`)

16-generation evolve per cycle; opponent alternates enemy/mirror 2:1; eval
depth alternates 48/96 matches; restart from seed every 5th cycle; admission
requires candidate > reigning + 4 chips **on the same fresh 160-match block**
(era-2 blocks start at seed 5,000,000); every cycle also benched vs baseline-v0
as a continuity axis. Cycle 16 crashed (evolve non-zero exit) and the series
correctly logged it and continued.

## 3. Results

| phase | cycles | vs v1 (chips/match) |
|---|---|---|
| admissions | 1 (+23.4), 3 (+15.1) | early wins |
| rejections | 4–15, 17–18 (14 scored) | drift to par |
| last 8 scored | 10–18 | −6.9 … +3.5, mean ≈ **−0.5** |

- **Final king: +15.1 vs baseline-v1, defended 14 challenges.** vs-v0
  continuity held throughout (candidates +25 to +80, typically ~+50).
- The cycle-1 → cycle-3 hall-of-fame swap is the same-block admission rule
  working as designed: on cycle 3's fresh block the cycle-1 king re-benched at
  only +4.3, exposing its +23.4 as block luck; the +15.1 candidate beat it
  there and took the crown.
- **Cycles 10 and 15 scored *exactly* 0** — the zero-sum signature of a
  candidate byte-identical to the enemy. Both were restart cycles seeded from
  baseline-v1: 16 generations produced nothing that beat the unmutated seed
  in-loop. Evolution wasn't just failing admission; late in the era it was
  failing to self-improve at all.
- Cycle wall-time roughly doubled vs era 1 (30–50 min vs 15–26). Consistent
  with v1 tables playing longer, more contested hands.

**Verdict as logged:** saturation — dial-tuning is exhausted against a
defending enemy; progress needs new capabilities, not new weights. That call
looks right: 14 consecutive rejections with the last 8 oscillating around
zero is a converged series, not an unlucky one.

The counter to a defending champion was **not more defense — it was more speed
and more push**: claims up, safety down again, push-through-threat up, threat
sensitivity *down*. Two readings, both probably true:

1. Against an opponent that folds, aggression is rewarded — the defender
   vacates pots, so the answer to defense is pressure. Consistent with the
   defender-tournament result.
2. The defense signal itself is weak, so evolution correctly discounts it.
   The threat scalar is in practice a meld counter (see
   THREAT-DETECTION-ANALYSIS-2026-08-27.md): ~0–5% detection on concealed
   winners, who are 34–47% of all winners. A dial wired to a half-blind signal
   can't earn much, and evolution priced it accordingly.

## 5. Caveats on the era-2 conclusion

- **+15.1 is a one-block point estimate.** Chips/match variance is huge (a
  single match can swing ±400; era 1's +70.2 deflated to ~+35-40 bias-free).
  The 14 defended challenges support "v2 ≥ v1"; the magnitude could
  plausibly be anywhere in roughly +5–15.
- **Possible specialization against v1.** The admitted king benched only +25
  vs v0 on its admission block while several *rejected* candidates hit +65–80
  vs v0. Head-to-head chains (v2 > v1 > v0) are not transitive strength
  proofs; if the goal is absolute strength, a periodic 3-way round-robin
  (v0/v1/v2 at one table, all chair rotations) would be a cheap guard.
- **The mirror cycles carried little information** in this era: mirror-trained
  candidates were admitted once (cycle 3) but mirror training was also the
  documented era-1 dead end. With a defending enemy available, the 2:1
  enemy/mirror mix may be worth revisiting toward 3:1 or 4:1 in era 3.

## 6. Where era 3 picks up

Era 3 (running now) is the direct response to the saturation verdict: same
king-of-the-hill structure vs baseline-v2, but with **new capabilities as
evolvable dials** rather than new weights on old signals — score awareness
(leadDefense / trailSwing / winFastLead), an evolvable hard fold
(foldThreshold — the owner explicitly wants the sim to find the line), feed
denial, and fitness switched from points to **chips**. Seeds at v2 + 0.3/0.5
on the new dials; blocks 7.5M+. Concealed-hand reading was explicitly
deprioritized per owner ruling (rare-massive unreadable; concealed-chow
answered with tempo), so the threat-model blindness documented in the threat
analysis stays a known, accepted gap for this era.
